Unit 3 — Motivation and Leadership

SBTE scope: motivation, McGregor X/Y, Maslow, Herzberg motivation-hygiene theory and leadership definition/ingredients/styles/theories. fileciteturn306file3

3.1 Motivation

Motivation refers to factors/processes that influence a person's direction, effort and persistence toward a goal. In management, motivation is studied to understand why employees engage with tasks and how organizational conditions influence performance.

3.2 McGregor Theory X and Y

Theory X represents assumptions that employees may dislike work, avoid responsibility and require close direction. Theory Y represents assumptions that work can be natural, people can exercise self-direction and responsibility can be developed. The theories describe contrasting managerial assumptions.

3.3 Maslow's Hierarchy

Maslow's model arranges needs from basic physiological needs through safety, social/belonging, esteem and self-actualization. In workplace cases, identify which need level the situation is addressing before suggesting a management response.

3.4 Herzberg Motivation-Hygiene Theory

Herzberg distinguishes hygiene factors, whose absence can create dissatisfaction, from motivators, which are associated with satisfaction and the content of work. Exam answers should state that the two categories play different roles.

3.5 Leadership

Leadership is the process of influencing people toward goals. Important ingredients include leader, followers, goals, situation and influence. Styles can include autocratic, democratic/participative and laissez-faire. Leadership theories explain leadership using different perspectives such as traits, behavior and situation.

Exam focusExplain all four motivation theories/concepts, compare X vs Y, draw Maslow hierarchy, distinguish hygiene vs motivators and compare leadership styles.
