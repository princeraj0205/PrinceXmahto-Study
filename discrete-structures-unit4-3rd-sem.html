4.1 Basic Concepts

A graph G=(V,E) has a set of vertices V and edges E connecting vertices. Graphs can model networks, routes and relationships. For an undirected graph, the degree of a vertex is the number of incident edges; a loop contributes twice to degree.

4.2 Types and Degree

Common types include simple graphs, multigraphs, directed graphs and weighted graphs. In an undirected graph, the sum of all vertex degrees equals 2|E| (handshaking principle).

4.3 Subgraph and Isomorphic Graphs

A subgraph uses a subset of vertices/edges of the original graph. Two graphs are isomorphic when there is a one-to-one correspondence between vertices that preserves adjacency. Compare vertex count, edge count and degree patterns as necessary checks.

4.4 Walks, Paths and Cycles

  • Walk: sequence of vertices/edges where repetition may occur.
  • Path: a walk with no repeated vertex under the usual simple-path definition.
  • Cycle: closed path whose start and end vertex coincide.

4.5 Eulerian Graph

An Euler circuit is a closed trail using every edge exactly once. For a connected undirected graph, an Euler circuit exists when every vertex has even degree; an Euler trail (open) exists when exactly zero or two vertices have odd degree.

4.6 Connectivity and Components

A graph is connected if every pair of vertices has a path between them. A disconnected graph can be split into connected components. In exam problems, identify components by grouping vertices that are mutually reachable.

Exam focusDegree calculations, graph classification, isomorphism checks, walk/path/cycle definitions, Euler conditions and connected components.
