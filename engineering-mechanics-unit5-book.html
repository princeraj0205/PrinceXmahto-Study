5.1 • Fundamental Concepts

Simple Lifting Machine

Definition: A lifting machine is a mechanical device used to lift or move a load by applying an effort, generally with mechanical advantage and/or a change in direction or distance of motion.

Real life mein heavy load ko directly lift karna difficult hota hai. Lifting machine ka main purpose human effort ko mechanically useful banana hai. Machine input side par effort receive karti hai aur output side par load ko move karti hai.

Basic Input–Output Concept

Figure 1 — Basic operation of a lifting machine

Why Do We Use a Lifting Machine?

1. Effort Reduction

Suitable machine arrangement se comparatively smaller effort se larger load lift kiya ja sakta hai.

2. Direction Change

Kuch machines effort ki direction ko convenient direction mein change karti hain.

3. Controlled Movement

Load ko controlled and gradual movement ke saath lift kiya ja sakta hai.

4. Mechanical Advantage

Machine geometry aur transmission ke through force multiplication provide kar sakti hai.

Load and Effort

Load (W)

Load machine dwara lifted/resisted useful external force hai. Usually load ko W se denote kiya jata hai.

Common SI unit: Newton (N)

Effort (P)

Machine ko operate karne ke liye applied input force ko effort kehte hain. Isse generally P se denote karte hain.

Common SI unit: Newton (N)

Mechanical Advantage (MA)

Mechanical Advantage tells us how many times the machine multiplies the applied effort in terms of load.
MA = W / P W = Load, P = Effort

Agar W = 1000 N aur P = 200 N hai, then machine ka MA = 5. Iska simple meaning hai ki load effort ka 5 times hai.

Important: Mechanical Advantage dimensionless quantity hai. Iski koi SI unit nahi hoti.

Interpretation of MA

Condition Meaning
MA > 1 Load is greater than applied effort; force multiplication occurs.
MA = 1 Load and effort are equal in magnitude.
MA < 1 Effort is greater than load; machine may provide speed/distance or other mechanical advantage.

Velocity Ratio (VR)

Definition: Velocity ratio is the ratio of the distance moved by effort to the distance moved by load in the same time.
VR = Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load VR is dimensionless

Agar effort point 500 mm move karta hai aur load 50 mm move karta hai, then VR = 500/50 = 10.

Why is VR usually greater than 1?

Many lifting machines mein effort ko comparatively larger distance move karna padta hai so that load comparatively smaller distance move kare. This arrangement helps in obtaining mechanical advantage.

Efficiency of Machine

Machine ki efficiency useful output aur input ke ratio ko percentage form mein express karti hai.
η = (MA / VR) × 100% η = efficiency in percent
MA = η × VR / 100 Useful when efficiency and VR are given
VR = 100 × MA / η Useful when MA and efficiency are given
Golden relationship: For an actual machine, efficiency is generally less than 100%, therefore MA is generally less than VR.

Applications and Advantages of Lifting Machines

  • Heavy loads ko manageable effort se lift karna.
  • Load movement ko controlled banana.
  • Mechanical advantage obtain karna.
  • Manual handling ko comparatively easier banana.
  • Workshops, garages, construction and material-handling situations mein use.
  • Specific application ke according force, speed, direction aur motion relationship obtain karna.

Law of Machine

For a particular machine under a given condition, the effort is represented by a linear relation with load:
P = mW + C m = constant/slope of machine relation, C = effort required at zero load

Here P is effort and W is load. m machine constant/slope ko represent karta hai, while C represents the intercept or no-load effort under the adopted linear model.

Determination of m and C from two observations

m = (P₂ − P₁) / (W₂ − W₁)

C = P₁ − mW₁

Meaning of the Law of Machine

Law of machine tells us that given machine ke operating range mein load badhne par required effort approximately linearly increase kar sakta hai. Graphical analysis mein isi relation ka important role hai.

5.2 • Machine Performance

Ideal Machine

Ideal machine woh theoretical machine hai jisme friction aur other energy losses ko zero maana jata hai.

Input = Output

Energy loss nahi maana jata.

Efficiency

η = 100%

MA and VR

MA = VR

η = 100%

MA = VR

Ideal Effort

Agar machine ideal ho, then actual frictional loss nahi hota. Load ko lift karne ke liye required theoretical effort ko ideal effort kehte hain.

Pᵢ = W / VR Pᵢ = ideal effort

Friction in Machine

Real machines mein moving surfaces ke contact, bearings, gears, ropes, screw threads, pulley surfaces etc. ke karan resistance develop hota hai. Is resistance ko machine friction ke context mein consider kiya jata hai.

Effect on Effort

Actual machine ko same load lift karne ke liye ideal machine se greater effort require ho sakta hai.

Effect on Efficiency

Frictional losses increase hone par useful output/input ratio decrease hota hai.

Effort Lost in Friction

Effort lost in friction = P − Pᵢ

Is quantity ko force terms mein express kiya jata hai. Agar actual effort 160 N aur ideal effort 120 N hai, then friction ke against additional effort = 40 N.

Maximum Mechanical Advantage

Law of machine:

P = mW + C

Mechanical advantage:

MA = W/P = W/(mW+C)

Very large load condition mein, practical limiting behaviour:

MAmax/limiting ≈ 1/m
Exam note: This limiting value is obtained from the machine-law model when W becomes very large. It should not be confused with the actual MA at an arbitrary finite load.

Maximum Efficiency

Since:

η = (MA/VR) × 100%

For a fixed machine VR, efficiency increases when MA increases. Under the ideal limiting model:

ηmax/limiting ≈ [1/(m × VR)] × 100%

Reversible and Non-Reversible Machines

Reversible Machine

A machine is called reversible when the load can, under suitable conditions, drive the machine backward after the effort is removed.

Standard textbook treatment commonly associates η > 50% with a reversible machine.

Non-Reversible / Self-Locking Machine

A machine is non-reversible when the load cannot drive the machine backward by itself under the considered condition.

Standard textbook criterion commonly associates η < 50% with a non-reversible machine.

Condition for Reversibility

η > 50% → Reversible

η < 50% → Non-reversible / Self-locking

η ≈ 50% → Limiting condition
Concept: Reversibility ka relation machine ke energy loss and geometry se hai. A machine with high resistance to reverse motion may be preferred where self-locking behaviour is useful.

Important Difference Table

Point Reversible Non-Reversible
Reverse motion Load may drive machine backward under suitable condition. Load does not drive machine backward by itself under considered condition.
Textbook efficiency criterion η > 50% η < 50%
Self-locking tendency Lower Higher
5.3 • Velocity Ratios

Velocity Ratio of Simple Lifting Machines

Is section mein syllabus mein specifically diye gaye lifting machines ke velocity-ratio relationships ko understand karna important hai. Formula yaad karne se pehle machine ke moving parts aur displacement relation ko samajhna better hai.

1. Simple Wheel & Axle

Wheel radius aur axle radius ke ratio se VR.

2. Differential Axle & Wheel

Two axle radii ke difference ka role hota hai.

3. Worm & Worm Wheel

Worm starts aur worm-wheel teeth ke basis par VR.

4. Crab Winch

Handle/gear train ratios combined hote hain.

5. Screw Jack

Handle circumference aur screw lead/pitch se VR.

6. Weston Differential Pulley

Two pulley diameters ka difference determine karta hai VR.

Machine 1

Simple Wheel and Axle

A simple wheel and axle consists of a larger wheel rigidly attached to a smaller axle. The effort is applied at the wheel and the load is connected to the axle.
Figure 2 — Simple wheel and axle

Working Principle

Jab wheel rotate hota hai, axle bhi same angular displacement se rotate karta hai because both are rigidly connected. Effort point wheel ke circumference par larger distance travel karta hai, while load attached to axle comparatively smaller distance travel karta hai.

Velocity Ratio Derivation

Let one complete revolution occur.

  • Distance moved by effort = circumference of wheel = 2πR
  • Distance moved by load = circumference of axle = 2πr
VR = 2πR / 2πr

VR = R/r

Applications

  • Hand-operated winding arrangements.
  • Simple lifting and pulling mechanisms.
  • Manual force multiplication systems.
Exam point: Simple wheel and axle ke VR mein wheel radius aur axle radius ka ratio use hota hai, diameter ratio bhi equivalent hai because 2 factors cancel.
Machine 2

Differential Axle and Wheel

Differential axle and wheel mein axle ke do different radii hote hain. Rope/chain arrangement aur unequal axle diameters ke karan small difference in circumference load-supporting system mein use hota hai.
Figure 3 — Differential axle and wheel: conceptual arrangement

Velocity Ratio

Standard arrangement mein one revolution of the wheel produces a difference in rope movement equal to the circumference difference of the two axle portions.

Distance moved by effort = 2πR

Relative distance available for load = 2π(R − r)

VR = R / (R − r)

Kuch textbook notation mein machine ko wheel-and-axle differential arrangement ke form mein define karke dimensions ko different symbols se express kiya jata hai. Isliye numerical mein given diagram ke labels ko carefully follow karna zaroori hai.

Important notation rule: Agar problem explicitly standard differential axle-and-wheel arrangement mein wheel radius ko R aur two axle radii ko r₁, r₂ deta hai, then displacement derivation should be done from the actual rope movement. Formula blindly substitute na karein.

Conceptual Advantage

Two axle radii ka difference small hone par load-supporting movement per revolution small hota hai, resulting in a comparatively high velocity ratio.

Machine 3

Worm and Worm Wheel

Worm and worm wheel mechanism mein a screw-like worm ek toothed wheel ke saath mesh karta hai. Worm ki rotation worm wheel ko rotate karti hai.
Figure 4 — Worm and worm wheel

Working Principle

  1. Effort is applied to rotate the worm.
  2. Worm threads engage with teeth of the worm wheel.
  3. Worm wheel rotates at a reduced speed.
  4. The output rotation can be used for lifting through a drum/rope arrangement.

Velocity Ratio

VR = Number of teeth on worm wheel / Number of starts of worm

For a single-start worm, one complete worm revolution advances the worm wheel by one tooth. For a two-start worm, the advance is two teeth per revolution. Hence the number of worm starts directly affects VR.

Example Interpretation

If worm wheel has 40 teeth and the worm is single-start:

VR = 40 / 1 = 40
Exam tip: Worm wheel ke teeth ko numerator aur worm ke number of starts ko denominator mein identify karna easy memory rule hai.
Machine 4

Single Purchase and Double Purchase Crab Winch

Crab winch ek geared lifting arrangement hai jisme handle ke rotation ko gear train ke through drum tak transmit kiya jata hai. Gear train speed reduce karta hai and torque multiplication provide karta hai.

Single Purchase Crab Winch

Figure 5 — Conceptual single-purchase crab winch

Single-purchase arrangement mein one gear pair transmission provide karta hai. Exact VR notation depends on how the handle radius, drum radius and gear teeth are labelled.

VR = (R/r) × (Tdriven/Tdriver)

Here R = effort handle radius, r = load drum radius, and gear-tooth ratio must be taken according to the actual driving/driven gear labels.

Double Purchase Crab Winch

Double purchase crab winch mein two gear pairs hote hain. Therefore total velocity ratio individual stage ratios ka product hota hai.

VR = (R/r) × (T₂/T₁) × (T₄/T₃)

In this notation T₁–T₂ first gear pair and T₃–T₄ second gear pair ko represent karte hain. Actual question mein gear labels alag ho sakte hain, so driving and driven gear identify first.

Single vs Double Purchase

Feature Single Purchase Double Purchase
Gear pairs One major gear pair Two gear pairs
Total transmission ratio One gear ratio × handle/drum ratio Two gear ratios × handle/drum ratio
Mechanical arrangement Comparatively simpler More stages of reduction
Typical use concept Moderate lifting arrangements Higher reduction applications
Machine 5

Screw Jack

Screw jack is a lifting machine in which rotary motion of a handle is converted into linear motion of a screw to lift the load.
Figure 6 — Screw jack

Working Principle

  1. Effort is applied at the handle.
  2. Handle rotation causes the screw to rotate.
  3. Thread geometry converts rotary motion into axial motion.
  4. The load-supporting head moves upward or downward.

Velocity Ratio of Screw Jack

Suppose handle radius/effective length is l and screw lead is p.

For one complete revolution:

  • Distance moved by effort = 2πl
  • Distance moved by load = p
VR = 2πl / p
Pitch vs Lead: Single-start screw mein lead = pitch. Multi-start screw mein lead generally pitch × number of starts. Numerical mein question mein jo quantity explicitly given ho usko carefully interpret karein.

Applications

  • Vehicle lifting arrangements.
  • Mechanical workshop lifting.
  • Controlled vertical lifting.
  • Applications where gradual movement and high force multiplication are required.
Machine 6

Weston's Differential Pulley Block

Weston's differential pulley block is a lifting mechanism using two pulleys of unequal effective diameters attached together and an endless chain arrangement.
Figure 7 — Conceptual Weston differential pulley block

Velocity Ratio

Standard Weston differential pulley arrangement mein one revolution of the upper differential pulley causes the two supporting chain portions to move through unequal distances. Their difference produces the lifting motion.

VR = 2D / (D − d)

Here D is the effective diameter of the larger upper pulley and d is the effective diameter of the smaller upper pulley.

Memory: Weston differential pulley → numerator mein 2D, denominator mein D − d.
Machine 7

Geared Pulley Block

A geared pulley block uses a gear transmission between the input chain/handle and the lifting pulley system so that the operator can lift a comparatively heavy load with controlled effort.
Figure 8 — Conceptual geared pulley block

Working Principle

  1. Input chain/handle causes the driving gear to rotate.
  2. Driving gear rotates the next gear in the train.
  3. Multiple gear stages can provide speed reduction.
  4. Output motion drives the lifting pulley arrangement.

Velocity Ratio Approach

Geared pulley block ka exact VR machine ke gear arrangement, number of pulley supporting strands and gear dimensions par depend karta hai. Therefore numerical problem mein diagram ke according derive karna safest approach hai.

Total gear ratio = Product of individual driven/driver gear ratios

Agar pulley arrangement ka own velocity ratio n hai aur gear train ratio G hai, then:

VRtotal = G × n
Important: Geared pulley block ke liye ek single universal numerical formula assume nahi karna chahiye without knowing the actual arrangement. Question mein given gear teeth and pulley geometry ko follow karein.
5.4 • Graphical Analysis

Graphs of Lifting Machine

Official syllabus mein following graphs included hain: Load vs Effort, Load vs Ideal Effort, Load vs Effort Lost in Friction, Load vs MA and Load vs Efficiency.

Graphing rule: Syllabus mein graph names “Load verses Effort” etc. diye gaye hain. Exam mein axes ko clearly label karein. Data table se points accurately plot karke smooth/appropriate trend line draw karein.

1. Load vs Effort

Law of machine:

P = mW + C

Isliye effort aur load ke beech linear relationship obtain hota hai for the applicable machine-law range.

Figure 9 — Load–Effort linear relationship

Graph Interpretation

  • Slope represents the machine constant m when axes are W horizontally and P vertically.
  • Y-intercept represents C.
  • Linear trend indicates applicability of the machine-law model.

2. Load vs Ideal Effort

Pᵢ = W/VR

If VR is constant, ideal effort is directly proportional to load. Therefore the ideal-effort graph passes through the origin.

Figure 10 — Ideal effort graph

3. Load vs Effort Lost in Friction

Effort lost = P − Pᵢ

For every load value, actual effort and ideal effort calculate karke difference plot kiya ja sakta hai.

4. Load vs Mechanical Advantage

MA = W/P

P = mW + C

MA = W/(mW+C)

At small load, C ka effect significant ho sakta hai. Load increase hone par MA limiting value ke approach kar sakta hai.

5. Load vs Efficiency

η = (MA/VR) × 100%

If VR is constant, efficiency ka variation MA ke variation ko follow karega. Real machine mein efficiency generally 100% se below hoti hai.

How to Draw Any Machine Graph in Exam

  1. Given observation table ko carefully read karein.
  2. Required dependent and independent quantities identify karein.
  3. Suitable scale choose karein.
  4. Both axes with quantity and unit label karein.
  5. Data points accurately mark karein.
  6. Trend ke according line/curve draw karein.
  7. Graph title and important intercept/slope mention karein.
Practice • TSO 5c & 5d

Long Solved Numericals

Har numerical ko exam-friendly fixed format mein solve kiya gaya hai: GIVEN → FIND → FORMULA → WHY THIS FORMULA → SUBSTITUTION → CALCULATION → ANSWER.

Numerical 1 — Mechanical Advantage

A machine lifts a load of 1200 N by an effort of 200 N. Find MA.

GIVEN W = 1200 N, P = 200 N
FIND Mechanical Advantage
FORMULA MA = W/P
WHY THIS FORMULA MA is defined as load divided by effort.
SUBSTITUTION MA = 1200/200
CALCULATION MA = 6
ANSWER: Mechanical Advantage = 6
Numerical 2 — Velocity Ratio from Distances

Effort moves through 600 mm while load moves through 60 mm. Find VR.

GIVEN Distance by effort = 600 mm; distance by load = 60 mm
FIND VR
FORMULA VR = distance moved by effort / distance moved by load
WHY THIS FORMULA VR is defined by displacement ratio.
SUBSTITUTION VR = 600/60
CALCULATION VR = 10
ANSWER: Velocity Ratio = 10
Numerical 3 — Efficiency

A machine has MA = 5 and VR = 8. Find efficiency.

GIVEN MA = 5, VR = 8
FIND η
FORMULA η = (MA/VR) × 100%
WHY THIS FORMULA Machine efficiency is MA-to-VR ratio expressed as percentage.
SUBSTITUTION η = (5/8) × 100
CALCULATION η = 62.5%
ANSWER: Efficiency = 62.5%
Numerical 4 — Find Effort from MA

A machine lifts 1500 N load and has MA = 6. Find effort.

GIVEN W = 1500 N, MA = 6
FIND P
FORMULA MA = W/P
WHY THIS FORMULA MA definition relates load and effort.
SUBSTITUTION 6 = 1500/P
CALCULATION P = 1500/6 = 250 N
ANSWER: Required effort = 250 N
Numerical 5 — Ideal Effort

Load is 1000 N and VR is 5. Find ideal effort.

GIVEN W = 1000 N, VR = 5
FIND Ideal effort Pᵢ
FORMULA Pᵢ = W/VR
WHY THIS FORMULA In an ideal machine MA = VR and MA = W/Pᵢ.
SUBSTITUTION Pᵢ = 1000/5
CALCULATION Pᵢ = 200 N
ANSWER: Ideal effort = 200 N
Numerical 6 — Effort Lost in Friction

Actual effort is 260 N and ideal effort is 200 N. Find effort lost in friction.

GIVEN P = 260 N, Pᵢ = 200 N
FIND Effort lost in friction
FORMULA P − Pᵢ
WHY THIS FORMULA Actual effort includes ideal effort plus additional effort due to losses.
SUBSTITUTION 260 − 200
CALCULATION 60 N
ANSWER: Effort lost in friction = 60 N
Numerical 7 — Law of Machine from Two Observations

For a machine, effort is 80 N at load 200 N and 140 N at load 500 N. Find the law of machine.

GIVEN W₁ = 200 N, P₁ = 80 N; W₂ = 500 N, P₂ = 140 N
FIND m and C; equation P = mW + C
FORMULA m = (P₂−P₁)/(W₂−W₁)
WHY THIS FORMULA m is the slope of the P–W straight line.
SUBSTITUTION m = (140−80)/(500−200) = 60/300
CALCULATION m = 0.2
FORMULA FOR C C = P₁ − mW₁
SUBSTITUTION C = 80 − (0.2 × 200)
CALCULATION C = 40 N
ANSWER: Law of machine: P = 0.2W + 40
Numerical 8 — Effort for a Given Load

A machine obeys P = 0.2W + 40. Find effort required to lift 800 N.

GIVEN P = 0.2W + 40, W = 800 N
FIND P
FORMULA P = mW + C
WHY THIS FORMULA Given machine law directly relates effort and load.
SUBSTITUTION P = 0.2(800) + 40
CALCULATION P = 160 + 40 = 200 N
ANSWER: Required effort = 200 N
Numerical 9 — MA from Machine Law

Machine law is P = 0.25W + 50. Find MA when W = 1000 N.

GIVEN P = 0.25W + 50, W = 1000 N
FIND MA
FORMULA P = 0.25W + 50; MA = W/P
WHY THIS FORMULA First actual effort is found from machine law, then MA from definition.
SUBSTITUTION P = 0.25(1000)+50
CALCULATION P = 300 N; MA = 1000/300 = 3.333
ANSWER: MA ≈ 3.33
Numerical 10 — Simple Wheel and Axle VR

Wheel radius is 250 mm and axle radius is 50 mm. Find VR.

GIVEN R = 250 mm, r = 50 mm
FIND VR
FORMULA VR = R/r
WHY THIS FORMULA One revolution gives effort distance 2πR and load distance 2πr.
SUBSTITUTION VR = 250/50
CALCULATION VR = 5
ANSWER: Velocity Ratio = 5
Numerical 11 — Wheel and Axle Efficiency

A wheel and axle has wheel radius 300 mm and axle radius 60 mm. If actual effort for a 900 N load is 200 N, find MA, VR and efficiency.

GIVEN R = 300 mm, r = 60 mm, W = 900 N, P = 200 N
FIND MA, VR, η
FORMULA MA = W/P; VR = R/r; η = (MA/VR)×100%
WHY THIS FORMULA These are the standard machine performance relations.
SUBSTITUTION MA = 900/200 = 4.5
CALCULATION VR = 300/60 = 5
EFFICIENCY η = (4.5/5)×100 = 90%
ANSWER: MA = 4.5, VR = 5, Efficiency = 90%
Numerical 12 — Worm and Worm Wheel

A worm wheel has 60 teeth and the worm is single-start. Find VR.

GIVEN T = 60 teeth, worm starts = 1
FIND VR
FORMULA VR = number of worm-wheel teeth / number of worm starts

Numerical 13 — Two-Start Worm

A worm wheel has 80 teeth and the worm is two-start. Find VR.

GIVEN T = 80, starts = 2

Numerical 14 — Screw Jack VR

Handle radius is 350 mm and screw lead is 7 mm. Find VR.

GIVEN l = 350 mm, p = 7 mm
FIND VR
FORMULA VR = 2πl/p

Numerical 15 — Screw Jack Efficiency

A screw jack has VR = 250 and lifts 5000 N with an effort of 40 N. Find efficiency.

GIVEN W = 5000 N, P = 40 N, VR = 250

Numerical 16 — Weston Differential Pulley

The larger pulley diameter is 200 mm and smaller pulley diameter is 180 mm. Find VR.

GIVEN D = 200 mm, d = 180 mm
FIND VR

CALCULATION VR = 400/20 = 20
ANSWER: Velocity Ratio = 20

A machine lifts 2000 N with an effort of 500 N. Its VR is 6. Find MA and efficiency.

GIVEN W = 2000 N, P = 500 N, VR = 6
FIND MA and η
FORMULA MA = W/P; η = (MA/VR)×100%
WHY THIS FORMULA MA comes from load/effort; efficiency comes from MA/VR.

CALCULATION η = (4/6)×100 = 66.67%
ANSWER: MA = 4; Efficiency ≈ 66.67%

GIVEN MA = 3, VR = 5

TSO 5g • Application

Selection of Relevant Lifting Machine

Machine selection mein sirf formula nahi, application requirement dekhna hota hai. Given purpose, required lifting behaviour, available effort, compactness, control and reverse-motion characteristics ko consider karke relevant machine justify ki ja sakti hai.

Purpose / Situation Relevant Machine Reason / Justification
Vehicle ko manually lift karna Screw jack Rotary handle motion ko controlled vertical screw motion mein convert karta hai.
Simple force multiplication Wheel and axle Different radii ke karan input distance aur output distance differ karte hain.
High reduction through screw gearing Worm and worm wheel Worm-tooth engagement large speed reduction provide kar sakta hai.
Manual heavy lifting with gear stages Crab winch Gear train input rotation ko drum rotation mein transmit karta hai.

Common Mistakes Students Make

01. MA and VR Mix-up

MA = W/P, while VR is distance-effort/distance-load.

02. Efficiency Formula Wrong

η = MA/VR × 100, not VR/MA × 100.

03. Ideal vs Actual Effort

Ideal effort frictionless condition ka theoretical value hai.

04. Gear Driver/Driven Confusion

Gear ratio mein pehle identify karein kaun driver hai aur kaun driven.

05. Screw Jack Pitch/Lead

Question mein single-start/multi-start condition check karein.

06. Graph Axis Missing

Axis quantity + unit + proper scale ke bina graph incomplete ho sakta hai.

07. Units Ignore Karna

Length ratios mein same unit use karein.

08. Machine Diagram Ignore Karna

Complex machines mein formula se pehle mechanism identify karein.

Core Quantities

Load: W

Effort: P

MA: W/P

VR: distance effort / distance load

η: MA/VR × 100%

Machine Law

P = mW + C

m = slope

C = intercept

Limiting MA ≈ 1/m

Ideal Machine

η = 100%

MA = VR

Ideal effort Pᵢ = W/VR

Friction effort = P−Pᵢ

Reversibility

η > 50% → reversible

η < 50% → non-reversible

η ≈ 50% → limiting condition

Wheel & Axle

VR = R/r

Worm & Worm Wheel

VR = teeth / worm starts

Screw Jack

VR = 2πl/p

Weston Differential

VR = 2D/(D−d)

Crab Winch

Overall VR = handle/drum ratio × gear ratios.

Five Graphs to Remember

  1. Load vs Effort → machine law P = mW + C.
  2. Load vs Ideal Effort → Pᵢ = W/VR.
  3. Load vs Effort Lost in Friction → P − Pᵢ.
  4. Load vs MA → MA = W/P.
  5. Load vs Efficiency → η = MA/VR × 100.
